
# Sprint Review and Retrospective

# How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I think the first step is understanding what the user is trying to accomplish, not just what features they are asking for. Something that sounds simple can turn into several questions once you start thinking about how to build it. During the SNHU Travel project, creating user stories helped me break down requests into smaller tasks and figure out what would make each one complete. Adding acceptance criteria was especially useful because it gave me something specific to work toward instead of guessing what the user expected.

# How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

When developing a program, I like to figure out the logic first and then work through the code one piece at a time. I've learned that trying to get everything right on the first attempt isn't always realistic. Testing as I go helps me catch problems early and make changes without having to redo everything. From Agile, I would definitely carry over short development cycles, regular feedback, and the flexibility to adjust when something isn't working. I would rather find out halfway through a project that something needs to change than discover it after everything is finished.

# What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

I don't think being a good team member is just about being good at coding or finishing your assigned tasks. You also have to communicate, follow through on your responsibilities, and understand how your work affects everyone else. One thing that stood out to me in this course was how easily a team can run into problems when requirements aren't clear or people make assumptions. I think being willing to ask questions, share what you know, and accept feedback is just as important as the technical work. At the end of the day, the goal is to deliver something that works, not just prove that you finished your part.
